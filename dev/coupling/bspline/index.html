B-spline I2O

B-spline moments are integrals against basis functions, not kernel samples. The coupling therefore needs the sampling matrix.

With sample-row matrices S_test and S_trial, the coefficient matrix is

\[W=S_{\mathrm{test}}^{-1}\,G\,S_{\mathrm{trial}}^{-\mathsf T}.\]

This is a description of the square, unreduced case. The buffered route also maps between raw and reduced bases after boundary-knot removal.

Dense route

MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BsplineKernelTranslator converts kernel samples to interpolation coefficients. MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BsplineI2OTranslationCollection pairs the stored coupling with MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BsplineDenseModulation.

The policy converts raw incoming moments to reduced storage before coupling, then expands the outgoing result. Test and trial conversion data are stored separately, so dense Petrov coupling can use different grids.

Toeplitz route

The raw coefficient matrix W is not itself Toeplitz. Move the sampling conversions onto the moments instead:

\[m_t^{\mathsf T}Wm_s =\left(S_t^{-\mathsf T}m_t\right)^{\mathsf T} G\left(S_s^{-\mathsf T}m_s\right).\]

Now the middle operation uses only kernel samples on a uniform grid. MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BsplineToeplitzModulation combines the basis conversions with FFTs. MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BsplineToeplitzI2OChannel holds the workspace's conversion scratch alongside the Toeplitz channel.

source raw moments
        ↓ basis conversion and FFT
frequency-domain coupling
        ↓ inverse FFT and output conversion
receiving raw moments

Level transfers always use raw moments. Reduction belongs to I2O only; it does not replace the bases used by I2I and O2O.

Choose either MultilevelKernelInterpolation.DenseI2OStrategy() or MultilevelKernelInterpolation.ToeplitzI2OStrategy() with the same buffered polynomial strategy. The constructors select the matching modulation policy; normal usage does not assemble these conversions by hand.
