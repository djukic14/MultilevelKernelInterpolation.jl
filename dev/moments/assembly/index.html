Assembling moment collections

MultilevelKernelInterpolation.TestMomentCollections and MultilevelKernelInterpolation.TrialMomentCollections build one collection per leaf. They select a moment operator, integrate it, and wrap the resulting matrices. Test and trial collections may differ in a Petrov map.

testcollections = MultilevelKernelInterpolation.TestMomentCollections(
    op, testtree, testleafpolynomials, testspace;
    quadstrat=BEAST.SingleNumQStrat(7),
)
trialcollections = MultilevelKernelInterpolation.TrialMomentCollections(
    op, trialtree, trialleafpolynomials, trialspace;
    quadstrat=BEAST.SingleNumQStrat(7),
)

These are construction calls, not per-multiplication operations. The normal map constructor makes them for you.

From an integral to a matrix entry

MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BEASTMomentAssembler visits the elements supporting each leaf's basis functions. At quadrature points it evaluates the box polynomials and calls MultilevelKernelInterpolation.integrand! with the basis-function data. Quadrature weights and BEAST's local-to-global assembly coefficients accumulate into the leaf matrices.

leaf polynomial + basis-function data
                  ↓ integrand!
          quadrature contributions
                  ↓ local-to-global assembly
       one matrix per moment component

Rows index the flattened tensor-product polynomial basis. Columns follow the basis-function indices in H2Trees.values(tree, leaf). Collections follow H2Trees.leaves(tree) order. Axis 1 varies fastest when flattening.

Choose quadrature or replace the assembler

assembly = MultilevelKernelInterpolation.MomentAssemblyStrategy(
    momentquadstrategy=BEAST.SingleNumQStrat(7),
    MomentAssembler=MultilevelKernelInterpolation.BEASTMomentAssembler,
)
strategy = MultilevelKernelInterpolation.InterpolationStrategy(op, space; momentassemblystrategy=assembly)

This quadrature setting is independent of near-interaction quadrature. The custom assembler page gives the two calls another assembler must implement. If only the integral changes, extending MultilevelKernelInterpolation.integrand! may be sufficient.

B-spline collections are converted to sparse matrices by MultilevelKernelInterpolation.postprocessmoment!, reflecting the local support of the basis.
